# Concept Planning

Use this reference only when the visual direction is not already fixed. It selects a rendering concept; it does not redesign the product contract.

## Start from the supplied tension

Extract the behavior, information relationship, brand posture, or emotional tension that makes the deliverable specific. Treat every page, state, viewport, action, and content assignment from the upstream contract or user as invariant.

Weak start:

```text
<fashionable style>, <palette>, <layout adjective>
```

Strong start:

```text
The visual system makes <supplied relationship or tension> perceptible so this
output communicates <its stated purpose>.
```

Do not turn a rendering concept into new navigation, product structure, copy, behavior, or code.

## Quarantine the predictable answer

Privately name the nearest generic completion: equal dashboard cards, oversized premium hero, arbitrary editorial offset grid, neon HUD, glass panels, pastel blobs, or another familiar default. These devices are not universally forbidden; they fail when they are the source of the idea rather than a consequence of it.

Move to a different organizing mechanism instead of merely banning the default.

## Diverge by mechanism

For high-ambiguity or high-stakes work, create two or three candidates that would still differ after removing color and typography. For a bounded output, one candidate plus one adversarial countercheck is sufficient. Useful lenses include:

- the supplied user ritual or decision;
- hierarchy, provenance, chronology, uncertainty, or contrast;
- one coherent material behavior such as folding, layering, stamping, tension, or reflection;
- progression, accumulation, reveal, inversion, subtraction, or scale shift.

Keep each candidate short:

```text
Thesis:
Feeling:
Signature relationship:
How the supplied content/function survives:
Why it belongs to this deliverable:
Main clarity or feasibility risk:
Renderer freedom:
```

## Select dialectically

For each serious candidate:

1. **Thesis** — state why it fits and could be memorable.
2. **Antithesis** — attack its clarity, specificity, feasibility, and restraint.
3. **Synthesis** — decide whether the weakness can be removed without killing the idea.

Reject a candidate when it is only a style label, transfers unchanged to another task, needs invented content, makes the supplied purpose harder to understand, or becomes legible only after exact geometry is prescribed.

Select one candidate. Do not average every survivor into style soup. A subordinate second idea is allowed only when it resolves one named weakness.

## Freeze the internal concept sheet

```text
Concept thesis: one sentence
Desired feeling: one phrase
Visual tension: one contrast
Signature move: one system-level gesture
Supporting moves: zero to two
Contract locks: supplied truth and continuity invariants
Creative latitude: decisions left to the renderer
Avoid: up to three likely concept-erasing failures
```

Keep exact regions, coordinates, counts, connector topology, and component trees out of the concept sheet unless the contract explicitly fixes them.

Translate the signature move one level upward before prompting. Describe the relationship or perceptual result the renderer should solve, not a literal artifact metaphor or predetermined wireframe. A renderer-facing concept is open enough when at least two materially different compositions could satisfy it.
